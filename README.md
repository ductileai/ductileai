## How it works

1. **Describe the job.** "Every Monday, find invoices more than thirty days overdue, write a polite reminder for each, and list them for me." Typed or spoken, in a browser.
2. **Read the plan.** In seconds, every step is named. Your companion never invents a step: it works from what it knows about your business and from each app vendor's own documentation.
3. **Watch a test run** on sample data. Nothing connected, nothing at risk.
4. **Approve.** The thinking is done. Fixed steps now run the job, every time, with no AI making decisions at run time.
5. **Read the record.** Every run says what it did, step by step, in words the owner and the auditor can both read.

## Ask it anything about your business

*"How much is unpaid across Stripe and Xero this morning?"* *"Which orders from this week have not shipped?"* *"What does this supplier's price page say today?"* Your companion reads your live apps, or the page at the address you give, and answers with the evidence. A question changes nothing. If you like the answer, one click makes it a job that runs every morning.

## How it is made

- **A runtime with no AI inside.** The plan runs as fixed, typed steps with a small formula language and no embedded scripts.
- **One shared rule set.** The designer in the browser, the server and the build pipeline validate a plan with the same code, so what passes on screen passes everywhere.
- **Verified connectors.** No app is added until its addresses, sign-in method and actions have been read in the vendor's current documentation. An app that cannot be verified is recorded as not built, with the reason.
- **Credentials as names.** Steps refer to a credential by name. The secret itself lives in the vault and is released to a step only while it runs.
